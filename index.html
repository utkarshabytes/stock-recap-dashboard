Best sellers

All products

User

The merchandiser or owner of a small fashion label with 60 products. They know sales and stock well but do not read complex charts every day. They decide what to reorder, what to put on sale and which category needs attention.

Use case

Opened on a laptop at the start of each month, in a five-minute check before placing reorders. The raw sheet has 19 columns, which hides one simple problem: the best sellers have run out while stock sits in slower categories.

Dashboard type

Operational. The user acts on each alert straight away, so monitoring and action share one screen. Best-seller cards and short lists keep it readable without chart training.

Information hierarchy

  • Most important: best sellers and the restock list.
  • Supporting: sales by price, sales by category, stock level, top colours.
  • Controls: period pills, category menu, filter chips, measure switch, alert switch, search, sorting.
  • Details on demand: the product panel with sizes and a suggested order.

Scanning pattern

F-pattern. The first bar runs across the top row from best sellers to the restock list. The second bar runs across the two charts. The icon rail and panel titles form the left stem. The side panel holds the totals for a final check.

Grid

  • Designed at 1440px wide, with a 72px icon rail.
  • 12 columns, 32px margins and 24px gutters.
  • Rows split 9+3 and 4+5+3, and the side panel spans both rows.
  • Colour swatches match the garment colour they name, so each colour keeps its swatch when filters change.
  • Text aligns left, numbers align right.

How the data was reduced

All 60 products stay, but 19 columns became 11. Sales, profit and stock state are calculated from those. Removed: product ID, SKU, subcategory, size list, launch date, season, initial stock and restocks.

Data limits

  • The file has no daily or weekly sales, so there is no timeline. The line chart runs across price, which has a natural order, and does not show time.
  • The file has no export date, so periods read "last 30 days".
  • Launch dates do not match sales. P031 launched on 26 September 2026, yet shows 43 units sold and none in the last 60 days. Launch date was left out.
  • Sales and profit use list price, without discounts or returns.
  • A product with no stock can show zero sales because it could not be bought.

What you can do

  • Filter: click a price band, a category bar or a colour. Every panel updates, and each filter shows as a chip you can remove.
  • Change the view: switch the period with the pills, and switch between sales, profit and pieces under the ring.
  • Browse best sellers: use the arrows, swipe the card, use the arrow keys, or click the cards behind.
  • Act on alerts: mark one product, or all of them from the alerts page, as ordered or for sale. Undo from the message or the orders button.
  • Look closer: click any product for stock by size, choose how many pieces to order and see the cost, then step to the next product.
  • Find: search from the top bar, or search, filter and sort the full product list.
  • Jump: click the ring to see the products with no stock, and the bell to open the alerts.

Stock rules used

  • Alerts and stock states always use the last 30 days, whichever period is selected.
  • Sold out: no stock left. The restock list shows those that also sold in the last 30 days.
  • Low: stock is less than the last 30 days of sales.
  • Too much: stock is three or more times the last 30 days of sales.
  • Clear stock: discontinued, with stock still left.
